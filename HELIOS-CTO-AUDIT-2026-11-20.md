# Project Helios CTO audit

20 November 2026

## Product verdict

Helios has a routed **12 V ↔ 24 V bench converter**, two working bench firmware images, and a substantial simulation record. It is still in pre-fabrication engineering. The repository contains one circuit family, the battery port. The production battery cabinet, full-power battery port, PV trackers, utility charger, three-phase inverter and transfer assembly are at design-study or requirements stage. A reliable 4–20 kWh/day product remains a viable design target. Whole-home autonomy at the Madurai pilot is a separate requirement and depends on measured usage and PV generation.

The immediate build decision is to converge on **one integrated bench revision**. Put the tested return planes, wider copper, selected switches and commutation network, sensing, bias and independent source disconnects into the same reviewed design. Close its switching and fault evidence on that revision. Start site measurement, pack interface definition and the three-phase single-line in parallel. Those inputs determine the full-power electrical design. Fabrication remains on hold under the current software-first plan. ## Product sizing and tradeoffs

### Energy, power and autonomy

Under the current assumptions, a 5.12 kWh cabinet delivers **3.69 kWh to the house** between a full charge and a 20% reserve, at 90% conversion efficiency. Two cabinets give about **7.37 kWh**, and four give **14.75 kWh**, before extra idle losses and aging. Four cabinets at an illustrative 80% retained capacity would give **11.80 kWh** on the same assumptions. That leaves **0.80 kWh** over the 11 kWh reference overnight load, before overhead. The modeled Madurai middle and heavy nights require 16.0 and 24.25 kWh of appliance energy. They need grid assistance, demand changes or a separately qualified larger bank. [Sizing and pilot cases](HELIOS-ARCHITECTURE.md#pilot-home-and-solar-self-sufficiency)

Scaling the published 100 kWp Madurai simulation's annual specific yield of 1,594 kWh/kWp, 5 kWp gives about 21.84 kWh/day. The 20 kWh reference day needs about 21.22 kWh of PV supply under the assumed storage penalty. That leaves roughly **0.61 kWh/day** before additional losses and curtailment. Continuous 25 W overhead consumes 0.60 kWh/day. Size with interval PV and load data, representative hot and cloudy weeks, reserve recovery and curtailment. Two 550 W modules add **1.10 kWp** of nameplate capacity if roof, string and tracker limits support them. [Madurai PV study, Table 5](https://academic.oup.com/ce/article/5/2/288/6291718) · [Helios energy assumptions](HELIOS-ARCHITECTURE.md#modular-platform-for-420-kwh-per-day)

| Healthy available cabinets | Tentative maximum from batteries alone | What the controller must enforce |
| ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1 | 2.5 kW | Match connected loads to the pack's live power and any phase limit. |
| 2 | 5.0 kW | Test unequal limits and redistribution after a cabinet drops. |
| 3 | 7.5 kW | Shed or supply the shortfall if the home was drawing 8 kW when one of four packs dropped. |
| 4 | 8.0 kW system target | Observe the 8 kVA aggregate and 4 kVA per-phase limits. Verify the per-phase peak and load transfer separately. |

These are architecture caps before site, temperature, pack voltage, time-at-power and state-of-charge derating. Real power also depends on load power factor. At 0.8 aggregate power factor, 8 kVA corresponds to **6.4 kW**. Publish an energy rating, a continuous and surge power envelope, and a per-phase load budget for every allowed configuration. Keep the 230/400 V, 50 Hz three-phase-with-neutral pilot and rotate the 4/2/2 kVA test across phases. Confirm service details at the site. [Power and phase targets](HELIOS-ARCHITECTURE.md#upper-configuration-for-three-phase-homes-with-four-room-acs)

The 10 kW utility input limit and 5 kW battery charge target describe different operating conditions. Using the design's 95% rectifier, 90% inverter and 95% charge-path assumptions, an 8 kW house load leaves only about **0.58 kW** for grid charging before auxiliaries. The controller and user interface should expose that live headroom and apply the site's per-phase import limits. [Grid charging budget](HELIOS-ARCHITECTURE.md#upper-configuration-for-three-phase-homes-with-four-room-acs)

### Architecture choices worth testing

| Choice | Recommendation and tradeoff |
| --- | --- |
| Battery-first energy flow | Keep the battery available as the buffer and reserve. Feed the shared DC link from PV when available and use the battery for the difference. This removes a charge/discharge conversion cycle for PV used immediately. Verify stable DC-link control through battery full, empty, disconnected, grid loss, load rejection and black start. |
| Independent cabinet control | Keep the independently managed reference ports. Each BMS reports time-valid charge and discharge limits and can isolate its cabinet. The system dispatches based on usable capacity, temperatures and loss. Simultaneous solar charging and house load are handled by net flow at the link. Compare a shared low-voltage bus only as a cost-down study with explicit compatibility, current sharing and aggregate fault-energy controls. |
| Base cost and standby | Quote four factory-populated ports against a factory-configured starter population using the same wall enclosure and approved expansion process. Compare lifetime cost, idle draw, service time and upgrade friction. Make unused channels genuinely low power where independent protection can stay available. Measure with a grid-forming output and the actual housekeeping loads. |
| Full-power converter efficiency | Give the battery port a bidirectional loss map over the actual pack and link voltages and a representative load histogram. Compare the existing dual-active-bridge control with the documented phase-shifted full-bridge reference at the same loads and temperatures. Compare circulating current, switching losses, magnetics, thermal headroom and sensing complexity. Add advanced modulation only if the duty-cycle-weighted savings justify the control burden. [Published converter comparison](circuits/REFERENCE-DESIGNS.md) |
| Inverter and DC link | Compare three coordinated phase modules with a purpose-built four-wire power stage. Evaluate neutral current and nonlinear loads, DC-link ripple, synchronization, single-phase economics and failure isolation. Select the link voltage from modulation headroom, parts, PV boost range, insulation and loss budgets. The 700 V simulation value is a study point. The two-panel PV startup requirement and the maximum cold array open-circuit voltage belong in the same MPPT acceptance matrix. |
| Utility import and legacy PV | Keep the physically import-only operating target and interlocked maintenance bypass. Define allowed legacy-PV retrofit configurations with the utility and installer. Include full batteries, lost meter/CT feedback, bypass faults, a welded contact and returning grid. Confirm no reverse flow at the meter under every allowed mode. |
| Dispatch and controls | Start with deterministic forecast, reserve, import limit and load-shedding rules. Make the offline fallback and hard BMS limits explicit. Compare imported kWh, reserve breaches and retained load against simple threshold and persistence baselines. Consider adaptive forecasting after collecting sufficient field data. Keep optimization outside the protection logic. |
| First household pilot | Compare a locally approved and documented matched inverter and battery system as a separate way to validate energy economics, load behavior and installation while the custom base is built. Keep the custom product's fault, efficiency and installation evidence tied to its own hardware. |

Competitor references are useful for packaging and per-module power claims. Anker's published X1 battery guide lists 5 kWh and 3 kW per module and names three-phase power modules. It is a reference for how to document compatibility and configuration limits. Libre Solar BMS C1 is a candidate reference for the 16S cabinet. The Infineon 3.3 kW reference gives a starting comparison for the full-power port. Reuse requires a license and revision record, a local design review and independent verification. [Anker X1 battery guide](https://support.ankersolix.com/s/article/Anker-SOLIX-X1-Battery-Module-X1-B5-H-X1-B5-H0-User-Guide) · [Helios circuit references](circuits/REFERENCE-DESIGNS.md)

For the savings case, replay the meter data against the site's actual import tariff and export credit. For each surplus kWh sent into storage, estimate the later import avoided after round-trip losses and subtract the credit forgone when that energy would have been exported. Include curtailment, equipment standby, battery aging, installation and service. Track solar self-consumption, the share of household load met by PV, overnight reserve, imports and unmet load separately. Tamil Nadu's rooftop portal documents additional-load and scheme-change workflows. Ask the installer and utility to confirm the pilot's metering scheme and retrofit route. [Tamil Nadu rooftop portal](https://www.tnebltd.gov.in/usrp/applyncfa.xhtml) · [Tamil Nadu rooftop billing FAQ](https://www.tnebltd.gov.in/usrp/faq.xhtml)

## Work to start now

1. **Converge the bench.** Define the single target revision and baseline a pass/fail matrix before routing. Include actual diode-loop inductance and fuse/disconnect coordination, driver-rail limits, commutation voltage and current, open-loop and fault control, and fail-safe startup. Close high-side vendor bias and hot faults on that revision before cloning the switching leg.
2. **Measure the pilot and price the requirements.** Start with at least two representative weeks of household and PV interval traces, including overnight cooling and high-load events. Capture faster per-phase starting events separately. Extend into other weather and seasons before claiming autonomy. Use the traces and installer quotes to choose which configurations get designed first.
3. **Freeze the rated interfaces and conformity path.** Draft the single-line, operating-mode table, per-phase and power budgets, site limits, and pack communications and disconnect contract. Ask an independent power-electronics reviewer, installer and test lab to agree on the qualification matrix early. Review India’s 2023 electrical safety regulations and distributed-generation connectivity regulations for the intended installation. The notified 2026 safety amendment specifies a commencement date of 1 April 2027. Have the lab determine which provisions apply at the expected installation date. For pack safety, IEC 62619 and IEC 63056 are references to assess with the lab. [2023 CEA safety regulations](https://www.cea.nic.in/api/dashboardReports/view-pdf/en/94cbf930-d997-4b91-b1df-17311c427569) · [2026 CEA safety amendment](https://www.cea.nic.in/api/dashboardReports/view-pdf/en/ab964031-51ad-4f59-b23e-5c242ed272d8) · [CEA connectivity regulations](https://cea.nic.in/regulations-subcategory/bfbde7c6-6263-4966-9fe0-14af26aa9cfb?lang=en) · [IEC 62619](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/64073) · [IEC 63056](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/29224)
4. **Constrain the development cost.** Keep one active bench revision and one production-intent port. Preserve older experiments by revision and index. Give the next port a loss budget, target BOM and qualified vendor or open design comparison before choosing topology. Assign named reviewers for power hardware, firmware and site acceptance before assigning a calendar release date.
